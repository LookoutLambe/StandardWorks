  1. 1

    כַּשֶּׁלֶגlike the snowבַּקַּיִץin summerוְכַמָּטָרand like the rainבַּקָּצִירin harvestכֵּןsoלֹא־נָאוֶהis not fittingלִכְסִילfor a foolכָּבוֹד׃honor

    As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool.

  2. 2

    כַּצִּפּוֹרlike a sparrowלָנוּדin flittingכַּדְּרוֹרlike a swallowלָעוּףin flyingכֵּןsoקִלְלַתa curseחִנָּםwithout cause(לא)[לוֹ]to himתָבֹא׃will come

    As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.

  3. 3

    שׁוֹטa whipלַסּוּסfor the horseמֶתֶגa bridleלַחֲמוֹרfor the donkeyוְשֵׁבֶטand a rodלְגֵוfor the back ofכְּסִילִים׃fools

    A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back.

  4. 4

    אַל־תַּעַןdo not answerכְּסִילa foolכְּאִוַּלְתּוֹaccording to his follyפֶּן־תִּשְׁוֶה־לּוֹlest you be like himגַם־אָתָּה׃you also

    Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.

  5. 5

    עֲנֵהanswerכְסִילa foolכְּאִוַּלְתּוֹaccording to his follyפֶּן־יִהְיֶהlest he beחָכָםwiseבְּעֵינָיו׃in his own eyes

    Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.

  6. 6

    מְקַצֶּהcuts offרַגְלַיִםthe feetחָמָסviolenceשֹׁתֶהdrinksשֹׁלֵחַhe who sendsדְּבָרִיםwordsבְּיַד־כְּסִיל׃by the hand of a fool

    He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage.

  7. 7

    דַּלְיוּhang limpשֹׁקַיִםthe legsמִפִּסֵּחַof the lameוּמָשָׁלand a proverbבְּפִיin the mouth ofכְסִילִים׃fools

    The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

  8. 8

    כִּצְרוֹרlike bindingאֶבֶןa stoneבְּמַרְגֵּמָהin a slingכֵּן־נוֹתֵןso is he who givesלִכְסִילto a foolכָּבוֹד׃honor

    As he that bindeth a stone in a sling, so is he that giveth honour to a fool.

  9. 9

    חוֹחַa thornעָלָהthat goes upבְיַד־שִׁכּוֹרinto the hand of a drunkardוּמָשָׁלand a proverbבְּפִיin the mouth ofכְסִילִים׃fools

    As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a parable in the mouth of fools.

  10. 10

    רַבthe Great Oneמְחוֹלֵל־כֹּלwho formed allוְשֹׂכֵרand rewardsכְּסִילthe foolוְשֹׂכֵרand rewardsעֹבְרִים׃transgressors

    The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors.

  11. 11

    כְּכֶלֶבlike a dogשָׁבthat returnsעַל־קֵאוֹto his vomitכְּסִילis a foolשׁוֹנֶהwho repeatsבְאִוַּלְתּוֹ׃his folly

    As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly.

  12. 12

    רָאִיתָdo you seeאִישׁa manחָכָםwiseבְּעֵינָיוin his own eyesתִּקְוָהthere is more hopeלִכְסִילfor a foolמִמֶּנּוּ׃than for him

    Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? there is more hope of a fool than of him.

  13. 13

    אָמַרsaysעָצֵלthe sluggardשַׁחַלthere is a lionבַּדָּרֶךְin the roadאֲרִיa lionבֵּיןamongהָרְחֹבוֹת׃the streets

    The slothful man saith, There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets.

  14. 14

    הַדֶּלֶתthe doorתִּסּוֹבturnsעַל־צִירָהּon its hingeוְעָצֵלso the sluggardעַל־מִטָּתוֹ׃on his bed

    As the door turneth upon his hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed.

  15. 15

    טָמַןburiesעָצֵלthe sluggardיָדוֹhis handבַּצַּלָּחַתin the dishנִלְאָהit wearies himלַהֲשִׁיבָהּto bring it backאֶל־פִּיו׃to his mouth

    The slothful hideth his hand in his bosom; it grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth.

  16. 16

    חָכָםis wiserעָצֵלthe sluggardבְּעֵינָיוin his own eyesמִשִּׁבְעָהthan sevenמְשִׁיבֵיwho answerטָעַם׃with discretion

    The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason.

  17. 17

    מַחֲזִיקone who takesבְּאׇזְנֵי־כָלֶבby the ears of a dogעֹבֵרpassing byמִתְעַבֵּרmeddlingעַל־רִיבin a quarrelלֹּא־לוֹ׃not his own

    He that passeth by, and meddleth with strife belonging not to him, is like one that taketh a dog by the ears.

  18. 18

    כְּמִתְלַהְלֵהַּlike a madmanהַיֹּרֶהwho shootsזִקִּיםfirebrandsחִצִּיםarrowsוָמָוֶת׃and death

    As a mad man who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death,

  19. 19

    כֵּן־אִישׁso is the manרִמָּהwho deceivesאֶת־רֵעֵהוּhis neighborוְאָמַרand saysהֲלֹא־מְשַׂחֵקam not jokingאָנִי׃I

    So is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?

  20. 20

    בְּאֶפֶסfor lack ofעֵצִיםwoodתִּכְבֶּה־אֵשׁthe fire goes outוּבְאֵיןand where there is noנִרְגָּןa whispererיִשְׁתֹּקceasesמָדוֹן׃strife

    Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth.

  21. 21

    פֶּחָםcharcoalלְגֶחָלִיםto burning coalsוְעֵצִיםand woodלְאֵשׁto fireוְאִישׁso a man of(מדונים)[מִדְיָנִים]quarrelsלְחַרְחַר־רִיב׃for kindling strife

    As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire; so is a contentious man to kindle strife.

  22. 22

    דִּבְרֵיthe words ofנִרְגָּןa whispererכְּמִתְלַהֲמִיםare as woundsוְהֵםand theyיָרְדוּgo down intoחַדְרֵי־בָטֶן׃the chambers of the belly

    The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

  23. 23

    כֶּסֶףsilverסִיגִיםof drossמְצֻפֶּהoverlaidעַל־חָרֶשׂon earthenwareשְׂפָתַיִםare lipsדֹּלְקִיםburningוְלֶב־רָע׃and an evil heart

    Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross.

    He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him;

    Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation.

  28. 28

    A lying tongue hateth those that are afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin.
